# Directive §13: Research Questions Matrix & Empirical Findings

This document explicitly tracks all 16 research questions posed in Section 13 of the **AI Mentalist Directive**, providing evidence classifications, repository references, and honest empirical findings.

Per project audit standards, every verdict is classified into an explicit evidence tier:
- **`human-measured`**: Falsified against real human participant trials in `data/sessions.db`.
- **`simulator`**: Measured in reproducible synthetic simulations (`simulator/`, `experiments/`).
- **`architecture / logic`**: Proven structurally in unit tests and deterministic state machines.
- **`literature`**: Grounded in published cognitive psychology or HCI research.
- **`assertion`**: Theoretical design intent, currently unmeasured.

---

## Research Questions & Experimental Matrix

| # | Directive Research Question | Evidence Class | Empirical / Design Verdict | Repository Evidence & Implementation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1** | **Which mentalism principles translate naturally into software?** | `architecture / logic` | **Bayesian belief updating, soft agreement fishing, choice architecture (visual saliency), multiple-outs reveal planning, and equivocation.** Soft likelihood updates prevent catastrophic elimination from noisy user input. | `services/hypothesis/tracker.py`, `services/language/response_signals.py`, `services/policy/reveal_planner.py` |
| **Q2** | **Which require a human performer and do not digitalize well?** | `literature` | **Subliminal hypnotic control, real-time micro-expression lie detection, and physical sleight-of-hand.** Unconstrained webcam micro-expression analysis lacks empirical reliability (Barrett et al., 2019) and fails under variable consumer lighting/hardware. | `research/mentalism-techniques.md` §5, `research/digital-translation.md` §5 |
| **Q3** | **Which techniques create the strongest discrepancy between perceived and actual information ($\Delta H_{\text{mystery}}$)?** | `simulator` | **Covert fishing statements paired with soft agreement updates and unasked corpus attribute reveals.** Under the modeled assumption $\kappa = 0.25$, visible interrogation ($I_{\text{visible}}$) is reduced by up to 66% relative to direct questions. (Note: 66% is $\kappa = 0.25$ restated, not an empirical human perception measurement; human calibration is in Phase 6). | `docs/magic-factor.md` §2, `docs/covert-fishing.md` §6, `experiments/run_magic_factor_eval.py` |
| **Q4** | **Which conversational patterns maximize information gain while minimizing perceived interrogation?** | `simulator` | **Observation $\to$ Cold-Reading Statement $\to$ Soft Agreement Confirmation $\to$ Progressive Narrowing.** Interleaving cold reads with direct turns prevents interrogation fatigue in simulation. | `services/policy/method_selection.py`, `services/language/fishing.py` |
| **Q5** | **Can psychological forcing work reliably in a digital interface?** | `simulator` | **Yes, in simulation via choice architecture (visual saliency + default positioning + delayed onset).** Simulated noise-concentration models yield 77%–90% force success rates without coercive countdowns or button locking. Real human priming rates remain to be measured. | `docs/choice-architecture.md` §4, `apps/web/src/lib/forcing.ts`, `tests/test_forcing.py` |
| **Q6** | **How much does UI design affect perceived mind reading?** | `assertion` | **Hypothesized to be significant.** Visual delayed emphasis onset (~1.2s glow), clean card layouts, and "peek-behind-the-curtain" disclosure panels ground the illusion in polished interaction design. Awaiting human trial Likert scores. | `apps/web/src/components/`, `docs/choice-architecture.md` §2 |
| **Q7** | **Does typing latency affect perceived inference?** | `architecture / logic` | **Yes, via evidence discounting.** Response latency acts as a reliability modulation: fast answers ($\le 2.0s$) retain 100% weight, while hesitant answers ($\ge 8.0s$) are discounted to 60%, refining the MAP hypothesis. Whether this discounting changes *perceived* mind-reading is unmeasured in humans (Phase 6). | `services/fusion/engine.py`, `docs/passive-signals.md` §4, `tests/test_fusion.py` |
| **Q8** | **Can the system infer latent traits rather than explicit answers?** | `assertion` | **Unanswered.** Pure reliability discounting is active (`services/fusion/engine.py`), but uncalibrated generative trait modules were pruned per Directive §20 to prevent unearned inference claims over nonexistent attributes. Generative latent inference remains an open research problem. | `services/fusion/engine.py` |
| **Q9** | **Can multiple weak signals outperform explicit questioning?** | `simulator` | **Yes, in synthetic simulation.** Fusing gaze dwell, response latency, and typing rhythm improves top-1 prediction accuracy by +0.8 to +3.0 percentage points across 4 effects on synthetic profiles. Whether this holds for real humans is unmeasured. | `docs/passive-signals.md` §4, `services/fusion/engine.py` |
| **Q10** | **Can reveal timing materially increase perceived accuracy?** | `architecture / logic` | **Mechanically verified in code.** Staging the reveal across progressive attribute ladder beats (prioritizing unasked corpus attributes) and injecting hesitation inside the calibrated doubt window ($0.60 \le P < 0.85$) converts simple guesses into dramatic deductions. Human impact awaiting §18 data. | `services/policy/reveal_planner.py`, `services/language/reveal_planner.py`, `docs/reveal-planning.md` |
| **Q11** | **How should the system behave when confidence is low?** | `architecture / logic` | **Use Multiple Outs: pivot to Category Cluster beats or Dual Deduction beats.** When no single candidate dominates ($P < 0.60$), naming the shared family or top two rivals prevents visible misses. | `services/policy/reveal_planner.py` §2, `docs/reveal-planning.md` §3 |
| **Q12** | **What is the minimum information needed to create a convincing reveal?** | `simulator` | **$\approx 3.5 - 5.5$ bits of actual entropy reduction**, paired with unasked corpus attribute reveals and $I_{\text{visible}} \le 2.5$ bits in simulated trajectories. | `docs/magic-factor.md` §3, `experiments/run_magic_factor_eval.py` |
| **Q13** | **Can reinforcement learning discover better performance strategies?** | `simulator` | **Yes, in simulation.** Contextual bandits trained in simulation over synthetic profiles discover state-dependent turn strategies matching hand-tuned heuristics. | `simulator/bandit.py`, `experiments/run_bandit_eval.py`, `docs/rl-policy.md` |
| **Q14** | **Can A/B experiments identify which methods produce the strongest "magic factor"?** | `architecture / logic` | **Apparatus complete, awaiting human data.** Double-blind A/B evaluation suite (`experiments/analyze_ab.py`, `services/api/main.py`) supports randomized assignment and Likert analysis, but contains 0 human rows in `data/sessions.db`. | `experiments/analyze_ab.py`, `docs/human-trials.md`, `services/api/main.py` |
| **Q15** | **Which methods generalize across cultures and languages?** | `architecture / logic` | **Bayesian belief updates, information gain math, choice architecture, and multi-out reveal structures.** Language templates adapt locally while core probabilistic state machines remain invariant. | `configs/effects/*.yaml`, `services/effects/models.py` |
| **Q16** | **Which effects disappear when participants know they are interacting with AI?** | `assertion` | **Supernatural/telepathic claims.** However, the mind-reading *illusion* created by information asymmetry, choice architecture, and strategic timing is hypothesized to remain effective when framed as computational intuition. | `README.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `docs/architecture.md` §1 |
